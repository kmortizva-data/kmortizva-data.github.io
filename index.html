Who I am

I am a mining and metallurgy engineer from Colombia, now on the Erasmus Mundus EMerald master's in Georesources Engineering across Belgium, France and Sweden. I graduated first in my class at Universidad Nacional de Colombia, with honours every semester along the way.

The critical metals crisis is easy to state and hard to escape. The world is electrifying: wind turbines, power grids, electric vehicles and the batteries behind all of them need copper, lithium, cobalt and rare earths in quantities we have never mined before. Recycling cannot close that loop yet, because you can only recycle what was bought decades ago, when the world bought far less of it. So most of tomorrow's metal must still come out of the ground, from deposits that grow poorer, finer and more chemically complex every year.

I believe the honest response to that is not bigger machines but better questions: innovation, curiosity and inventiveness applied at every step of the process. That is why, alongside the master's, I have been training myself through independent projects in machine learning and other engineering techniques that a doctorate or an industrial project will end up needing.
