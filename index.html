Froth

Maps a scientific field by meaning, so a literature review stops being five cherry picked papers.

Feed
A topic, and every paper OpenAlex has on it. Up to 10,921 in one mapped corpus.
Separation
Each paper becomes a vector by meaning, not by keyword (SPECTER). Density clustering finds the subtopics. Three views draw them: bubbles, a force directed network, and a reading guide.
Concentrate
44 named subtopics, the papers that bridge between them, and a logbook you fill as you read.

Assay

42% of the papers drawn share no link at similarity 0.75

Known, measured, and not hidden: at the current threshold nearly half the drawn nodes sit unconnected. Before the last round it was 36%, so this got worse, not better.

Silica in an iron flotation plant

Can a model call the concentrate grade before the lab does? I spent ten modules finding out that it cannot, and why.

Feed
737,453 rows and 24 sensors from a real iron ore flotation plant, March to September 2017.
Separation
Trees, forests, gradient boosting, a neural network, and lag features. Then, crucially, a persistence baseline to compare them against.
Concentrate
A verdict, 31 written notes and an 89 page volume.

Assay

the model adds +0.009 R² over doing nothing

Persistence, meaning "assume it stays where it is", scores R² 0.632 at one hour. The trained model scores 0.641. Past five hours both go negative. At twelve hours the model is actively worse than the baseline.

  • pandas
  • scikit-learn
  • gradient boosting
  • SHAP
  • LaTeX

Concentra

Turns the public syllabus of a course into a study sheet you can actually consult later.

Feed
The publicly visible syllabus of a Coursera course. One request, cached, nothing behind the login.
Separation
The module map is generated from the syllabus, never typed by hand. Around it I write the glossary, the recipe, the pitfalls and the review questions, with analogies from metallurgy.
Concentrate
One self contained HTML sheet per module, offline, dark and light, and it prints to PDF cleanly.

Assay

23 rounds of correction for the first course alone

Course 3 took 23 passes before it was right. The procedure that came out of that is written down in MANUAL.md, and three automated checks now have to pass before anything is committed. Skipping it means doing the work three times.

bg-remover

Cuts the background out of a photo on your own machine, and lets you repair the edges the model got wrong.

Feed
A 12 megapixel phone photo, downscaled once to 2000 px on the long side.
Separation
A segmentation model returns only the alpha mask, never a finished cutout. The browser holds the original pixels, the model's mask and a working copy as three separate layers.
Concentrate
A PNG with real transparency, and two brushes that can actually fix an edge.

Assay

the first cut takes 13 to 15 seconds, every one after that takes 2

DirectML compiles its GPU shaders on the first inference. Nothing is broken, but a user cannot tell a fifteen second wait from a hang, so the overlay counts the seconds out loud instead of spinning silently.

  • ONNX Runtime
  • DirectML
  • vanilla JavaScript
