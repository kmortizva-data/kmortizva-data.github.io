The brief and the terrane

Gold in a Proterozoic terrane. What is asked, what the file holds, and why the analysis is modular.

A file of 1,333 records carrying the geochemistry of 79 variables, elements and oxides, from gold to SiO2, each with a longitude and a latitude. That is everything there is at the start. The question this project puts to that file: where is the gold, and how far can the map we draw be trusted?

This chapter introduces the terrane, the file and the architecture of the analysis. The chapters after it drag in reality: censored data, duplicates, a distribution that is anything but normal, and finally a kriging map that confesses its own uncertainty.

The terrane

The data comes from a Proterozoic terrane in eastern South Australia, Proterozoic meaning rocks between 2,500 and 541 million years old, ancient even by a geologist's standards. The area covers roughly 62,000 km², about the size of Lithuania, and is known for its metallogenic variety: copper, uranium, thorium. This analysis commits to a single question, the one about gold (Au).

The map above deserves a minute. The background colours are geological units, 166 of them, and the dots are samples, coloured by their gold content in ppb: parts per billion, grams of gold per thousand tonnes of rock. Two things stand out before knowing any geostatistics at all: the red dots (more gold) do not fall just anywhere, and the sampling concentrates along corridors rather than a regular grid. Both observations will chase us through the whole project.

The architecture: modular on purpose

The analysis was built as a sequential modular structure: cleaning, exploration, variography and kriging are separate pieces chained together. That is not cosmetics. It means the target element can be swapped, from Au to Pr or Nd, say, without rewriting the analysis, and that when something breaks, it breaks inside a named piece rather than somewhere in a thousand-line monolith.

  • Clean first: inconsistent coordinates, censored data, spatial duplicates.
  • Explore next: how gold is distributed and what it travels with.
  • Model last: spatial continuity (the variogram) and estimation (kriging).

Order matters. Every cleaning decision changes what the variography can see, and every variography decision changes what kriging can estimate. A mistake in the first link reaches the final map dressed up as geology.

What stands established

A file of 1,333 records and 79 variables over 62,000 km² of Proterozoic rock, one question (where is the gold?) and a piecewise architecture to answer it. The next chapter opens the file and finds that, before mapping anything, something must be done about what is missing, because in some variables, more than 99% is.
