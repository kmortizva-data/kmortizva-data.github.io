What this module solves

Eleven ten-thousandths. And not for lack of room to improve: Course 5 tried four families of model, tuned 88 combinations of knobs and added an unsupervised segmentation, and the best result came from logistic regression, which was already there in Course 4.

What that means, and what it does not

It does not mean the courses were useless. It means three concrete things:

  1. The ceiling is set by the data, not by the tool. In Waze the file does not say why anyone left, so no model can get past that.
  2. Knowing you have reached the ceiling is worth as much as climbing. Course 5 did not improve the result: it proved it could not be improved, and that saves six months.
  3. The most useful finding of the four courses came from the simplest one. The 461 users who drive more than a thousand kilometres in a day were found by Course 2 looking at a histogram, and neither the analysis of Course 3 nor the models of 4 and 5 had flagged them.

That is the arc of the whole certificate, and it is what a portfolio has to tell: not the most advanced technique you can use, but that you know when it stops adding anything.

Terms

Glossary

  • Certificate badge. The verifiable credential claimed on finishing, linked from a professional profile.
  • Comparability. That two results can be put side by side. It demands the same question, the same data and the same split.
  • Data ceiling. The limit of accuracy imposed by the information available, above which no model can climb.
  • Negative result. An analysis that proves something cannot be done or does not happen. It is a deliverable, not a failure.
  • Portfolio. The selection of work you show. Twelve analyses are not a portfolio; three well told are.

Code

The code, in parts

Step 1. Check whether two courses can be compared

Before any synthesis, the mechanical question: did they measure on the same rows?

import json
from pathlib import Path

def leer(curso, caso):
    ruta = Path(f"projects/{curso}/{caso}/04_reports/model_results.json")
    return json.loads(ruta.read_text(encoding="utf-8")) if ruta.exists() else None

def mejor_auc(datos):
    bloque = datos.get("test") or datos.get("metrics_050") or {}
    aucs = [v["auc"] for v in bloque.values() if isinstance(v, dict) and "auc" in v]
    return max(aucs + [bloque["auc"]]) if "auc" in bloque else (max(aucs) if aucs else None)

for caso in ("waze", "tiktok", "automatidata"):
    antes, despues = leer("curso4", caso), leer("curso5", caso)
    prueba_antes = antes["split"]["test_rows"]
    prueba_despues = despues["split"]["test_rows"]
    if prueba_antes != prueba_despues:
        print(f"{caso:14} NO comparable: {prueba_antes} filas contra {prueba_despues}")
    else:
        ganancia = mejor_auc(despues) - mejor_auc(antes)
        print(f"{caso:14} comparable sobre {prueba_antes} filas   "
              f"el Curso 5 ganó {ganancia:+.4f} de AUC")
line by line
datos.get("test") or datos.get("metrics_050") or {}
Three alternatives chained with or: it takes the first one that is not empty. The courses saved their metrics under different names, and this absorbs that without an if per course.
isinstance(v, dict) and "auc" in v
Filters the values that are dictionaries with an AUC inside, skipping the ones that are bare numbers. Without the first check, looking for a key inside a number raises an error.
aucs + [bloque["auc"]]
Joins the two ways it can be stored: one per model, or a single one loose at the top. Adding lists concatenates them.
antes["split"]["test_rows"]
The number of rows in the test set. It is the check that decides everything: if they do not match, the two numbers measure different things and comparing them is inventing an improvement.
{ganancia:+.4f}
The + forces the sign even when it is positive, which here is exactly what has to be seen.
prints
waze           comparable sobre 3575 filas   el Curso 5 ganó +0.0011 de AUC
tiktok         NO comparable: 600 filas contra 3817
automatidata   NO comparable: 5634 filas contra 3041

Three lines of output and the whole certificate summed up.

Two of three chains cannot be compared, and saying so is more honest than putting the six numbers in a table and letting the reader draw conclusions that do not hold.

And in the one that can: +0.0011.

Traps

Watch out

  • **Comparing two results without checking that they measure the same thing is the easiest trap to fall into and the hardest to detect by reading.** It is checked with one line of code.
  • A portfolio is not all your work. Twelve analyses published without a criterion say less than three chosen ones.
  • The badge is not the portfolio. One says you attended; the other, what you can do.
  • Do not choose the projects for the model. The ones of yours that represent you best are the ones that deliver a "cannot be done" with evidence.
  • Reaching the data ceiling is a result. Course 5 did not improve Waze: it proved it could not be done, and that saves months.
  • And do not fall in love with the newest tool. In the only comparable chain, the regression from the previous course won.
Analogy

Metallurgical bridge

You have lived this already, and it has a name: the learning curve of a plant.

In the first year recovery goes up by the shovelful. You adjust the grind, correct the dosing and gain three points. In the second year, with an advanced control model and a serious test campaign, you gain half a point. In the third, with the best there is on the market, you gain a tenth and there is an argument about whether it is real.

It is not that the tools get worse. It is that the easy part was taken in the first year, and what is left is limited by the ore, not by the circuit.

At the plantIn these four courses
The three points of the first yearThe 461 impossible drivers, seen in a histogram
The half point of advanced controlThe regression of Course 4, AUC 0.7368
The debatable tenthThe trees of Course 5, +0.0011
The ceiling of the oreThe file does not say why anyone left

And the practical conclusion is the same in both places, and it is the one that separates an engineer with judgement from one with a catalogue: knowing you have reached the ceiling of the ore is worth more than another test campaign, because the campaign costs six months and the ceiling does not move.

A more complicated model on data that does not contain the answer is exactly that: an expensive campaign to confirm what was already known.

Review

Review

Why can only one of the three chains of analysis be compared across courses?

Because comparing requires that the two results measure the same thing on the same rows, and in two of the three scenarios Course 5 changed the question. In TikTok it went from predicting whether the account was verified to whether the video was a claim, and in Automatidata from estimating the fare to predicting the tip, so their test sets have 600 against 3,817 rows and 5,634 against 3,041. Only Waze kept the objective and the split, with the same 3,575 rows in both courses, and there the comparison means something. The good part is that this check depends on nobody's judgement: it is comparing two numbers and it takes one line.

A whole course of machine learning gained 0.0011 of AUC. Was it useless?

No, and confusing those two things is the mistake the module wants to avoid. Course 5 tried four families of model, tuned 88 combinations of knobs and added a segmentation, and the best result came from the regression that already existed. That does not say the new tools are useless: it says that in this file the ceiling is set by the data, because it contains no variable that explains why anyone left. And proving the ceiling has been reached is a deliverable, because it stops someone from spending six months trying to climb.

Why did the best finding of four courses come from the simplest one?

Because it was looking at something else. Course 2 modelled nothing: it drew distributions, and on drawing the kilometres per driving day 461 users appeared above a thousand kilometres a day, which is physically impossible. The later courses worked on that same column without seeing it, because a model does not look at the distribution of its variables, it uses them. It is the lesson that runs through the whole library: the impossible is not found by any statistical rule, it is found by someone looking at the picture with knowledge of the domain.

Which projects would you choose for a portfolio and why are they not the most technical?

The three that prove how you work, not what you can execute: Waze from Course 2, because it found what four courses did not see; TikTok from Course 5, because it teaches to distrust a 99.6% and to fix a control before looking at the result; and Automatidata from Course 5, because it delivers a "cannot be done" with evidence. Anyone can show a well-tuned random forest; knowing that an enormous signal was an artefact of how the file was generated, not.

What is the difference between the certificate badge and the portfolio?

The badge certifies that you completed the program, and anyone who completes it can say that. The portfolio shows what you do with it, and that is where one person is told from another. The badge gets claimed because it takes ten minutes and is linked from the profile, but the conversation that gets an interview comes from the portfolio: from a question someone had, a number that can be defended and a limit written before anyone finds it for you.
