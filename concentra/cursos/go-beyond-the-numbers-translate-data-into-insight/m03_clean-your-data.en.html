Clean your data

What to do with what is missing, what is repeated and what is out of range. Nine grades worked by hand show that a single extreme value moves the mean 3.33 points and the median only 1, and that there are two quartile conventions that give different numbers and the same verdict.

Context

What this module solves

The previous module ended with a file you can now read: the types are right, the dates are dates and you know where the gaps are. This one answers the next question, which is the uncomfortable one: and now what do I do with them?

And it is uncomfortable because almost no answer is automatic. Deleting a row changes the denominator of every percentage you calculate afterwards. Filling a gap with the mean invents a value nobody measured. Removing an extreme value may be throwing away precisely the finding.

The rule that governs the whole module is this: every cleaning decision is taken with a criterion and written down with its count. There is no correct cleaning, there is documented cleaning.

Example

Before the theory: a toy example

Nine grades of a lot, in percent, already sorted:

3   5   7   9   11   13   15   17   40

Eight values that are more or less alike and a 40 that stands out. The question of the module is not whether the 40 bothers us, it is what to do with it, and for that a number has to be put on it.

Step 1. The interquartile range criterion

The set gets split into four. With nine values, the median is the fifth, the 11.

numpy calculates the quartiles by interpolating, and with nine values they fall right on top of two data points:

Q1 = 7        Q3 = 15        IQR = 15 − 7 = 8

And the fences are placed a mile and a half of IQR from each quartile:

valla baja = 7 − 1,5 × 8 = 7 − 12 = −5
valla alta = 15 + 1,5 × 8 = 15 + 12 = 27

Anything outside −5 to 27 is a candidate. Only the 40 is.

Step 2. The other convention, which gives other numbers

If you do it by hand the way it is taught in many places, taking the median of each half and leaving out the central value:

mitad baja: 3, 5, 7, 9      →  Q1 = (5 + 7) / 2 = 6
mitad alta: 13, 15, 17, 40  →  Q3 = (15 + 17) / 2 = 16
IQR = 10                    →  valla alta = 16 + 15 = 31

Q1 is 6 instead of 7, Q3 is 16 instead of 15 and the fence moves from 27 to 31. With the same nine numbers.

It is not that one is wrong: they are two different conventions and there are more. Here both agree on the verdict, which is the usual thing, but if a value falls between 27 and 31, your conclusion depends on which formula your library used. That is why you say which one was used.

Step 3. How much damage the 40 does, measured

Before deciding, look at what that value does to each summary:

With the 40Without the 40How much it moves
Mean13.3310.003.33
Median11101

A single value out of nine moves the mean more than three points and the median one. There it is, with numbers, why the median is called robust and why in dirty data it gets reported before the mean.

Step 4. And even so, the formula does not make the decision

The criterion says the 40 is a candidate. What to do with it depends on something that is not in the table:

  • If the lot came from a known high-grade vein, that 40 is the most valuable data point in the set and it stays.
  • If the laboratory confused percent with parts per thousand, it is an error and **it gets corrected or removed**.
  • If there is no way of knowing, it gets clipped to the value of the fence and it is said that this was done.

Statistics flags candidates. Whoever knows the ore decides.

Terms

Glossary

  • Missing value. The empty cell, which pandas writes as NaN. It is not a zero.
  • Impute. Fill a missing value with an estimate: the mean, the median, the previous value or the most frequent one. It invents a value, so it is always declared.
  • Missing at random. That the probability of being missing does not depend on what the value would be. It is the condition that allows removing those rows without biasing.
  • Duplicate. A repeated row. It can be a loading error or two legitimate identical facts, and you have to find out which before deleting.
  • Outlier. A value far from the rest.
  • Quartile. The three values that split the sorted set into four equal parts. The second one is the median.
  • Interquartile range. The distance from Q1 to Q3, where the central half of the data lives. IQR for short.
  • 1.5 × IQR rule. The usual criterion for flagging outlier candidates: outside Q1 − 1,5 × IQR or Q3 + 1,5 × IQR.
  • Clip. Lower the extreme values to a cap instead of removing them, keeping the row. Also called winsorising.
  • Robust. A statistic that an extreme value barely moves. The median is, the mean is not.
  • Label encoding. Turning categories into integers. It is only valid if the categories have a real order.
  • One-hot encoding. Turning each category into its own column of ones and zeros. It is the right thing when there is no order.
  • Input validation. Checking on reading that the data meets the rules it has to meet: ranges, types and allowed values.
  • Personally identifiable information. Data that identifies a person: name, email, phone, exact coordinates. PII for short.
Method

Step by step

Step 1. With what is missing, first ask why it is missing

Before choosing a technique. A sensor that went off and an answer people avoid giving are not treated the same, even though both leave the same empty cell.

SituationWhat to do
Missing at random and fewRemove those rows, saying how many
Missing at random and manyImpute, saying with what and why
Not missing at randomNeither remove nor impute in silence: the absence is the data
A whole column is missing almost alwaysRemove the column, not the rows

And the check that decides between the first and the third: compare the group that is missing the value with the rest. If they look alike, it can be removed. If not, removing it changes whom the analysis describes.

About imputing, three options and when:

  • The median, if the column has extremes. It is the default option in dirty data.
  • The mean, if the column is symmetric and clean.
  • The previous value, if it is a time series and the phenomenon changes slowly.

All of them shrink the variability of the data, because they put central values in. With many missing values, that stops being harmless.

Step 2. With duplicates, find out before deleting

A repeated row is not always an error. Two identical readings from the same sensor at two different instants are two facts, not one.

The useful question is which combination of columns should be unique: the lot identifier, or the pair of identifier and date. That is called the key, and duplicates are looked for on it, not on the whole row.

Step 3. With outliers, sort by value and not by name

The previous module already taught that a number stored as text sorts wrongly. Here comes the version with categories: sorting by name and sorting by value are different things.

If you sort the shifts alphabetically you get "day, night"; if you sort them by their mean recovery, they may come out the other way round. The chart sorted by magnitude almost always tells the story better than the one sorted alphabetically, and it is a one-line change.

And with the extreme values, the three legitimate answers of the toy example: keep it, clip it or remove it. None of them is "delete it because it spoils the chart".

Step 4. Turn categories into numbers, with the rule you already know

WhenWhat is used
The categories have an order (low, medium, high)Label encoding: 0, 1, 2
They have no order (oxide, sulphide, mixed)One-hot: one column of ones and zeros per level minus one

Giving 1, 2 and 3 to three minerals with no order tells the model that the third is triple the first and that the distances between them are equal. Nobody has claimed that.

And before encoding, normalise the text: oxido, Oxido and OXIDO are three different categories for the computer and a single one for you.

Step 5. Validate the input, which is what saves repeating the work

Check the rules when reading the file, not when the result looks odd. Four checks that cover almost everything:

CheckExample
RangeA grade between 0 and 100; a recovery that cannot be negative
TypeThe date is a date, the grade is a number
Allowed valuesThe shift can only be day or night
UniquenessNo two rows with the same lot identifier

Written once, it runs always. It is the difference between discovering a problem when opening the file and discovering it in the meeting.

Step 6. And the people behind it

If the data describes people, there are obligations that do not depend on the analysis:

  • Anonymise before working, not before publishing.
  • Collect only what is needed. A value you do not have cannot leak.
  • Beware of what identifies without a name: a postcode with a date of birth and a sex identify a lot of people without needing a name.
  • Exact coordinates are identifiable, and in a trips dataset they are someone's home.

Step 7. Other transformations, when the scale gets in the way

Three that appear constantly:

  • Logarithm, when the data spans orders of magnitude. It turns a cloud squashed against the axis into something readable.
  • Normalise or standardise, when you are going to compare variables in different units.
  • Bin into bands, when what matters is the category and not the exact value. Information is lost in exchange for readability, and you have to know that this is being paid.
Code

The code, in parts

Step 1. The quartiles and the two conventions

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

grades = np.array([3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15, 17, 40], float)

q1, q3 = np.percentile(grades, [25, 75])
iqr = q3 - q1
print(f"numpy   Q1 {q1:g}   Q3 {q3:g}   IQR {iqr:g}   valla alta {q3 + 1.5 * iqr:g}")

low, high = grades[:4], grades[5:]
q1b, q3b = np.median(low), np.median(high)
print(f"a mano  Q1 {q1b:g}   Q3 {q3b:g}   IQR {q3b - q1b:g}   "
      f"valla alta {q3b + 1.5 * (q3b - q1b):g}")
line by line
np.percentile(x, [25, 75])
Asks for two percentiles at once and returns both. The square brackets are a list, and that is why they are caught in two names.
:g
A format that drops the zeros and the decimal point when they are not needed: it writes 7 instead of 7.0.
grades[:4]
The first four elements. In Python the slice goes up to the indicated index without including it.
grades[5:]
From the sixth onwards, skipping the fifth, which is the median. That is how the two halves are separated leaving out the central value, which is the other convention.
prints
numpy   Q1 7   Q3 15   IQR 8   valla alta 27
a mano  Q1 6   Q3 16   IQR 10   valla alta 31

The same nine numbers and two different fences, 27 and 31. Here both flag only the 40, but a value of 29 would be an outlier with one and normal with the other.

Step 2. Measure how much the outlier moves each summary

without = grades[grades < 40]

print(f"con el 40:  media {grades.mean():.2f}   mediana {np.median(grades):g}")
print(f"sin el 40:  media {without.mean():.2f}   mediana {np.median(without):g}")
line by line
grades < 40
Returns a list of trues and falses, one per element. It filters nothing on its own.
grades[grades < 40]
Using that list of trues and falses inside the brackets keeps the elements where there is a true. It is the standard filtering of numpy and of pandas.
:.2f
Two fixed decimals, so the two means can be compared column against column.
prints
con el 40:  media 13.33   mediana 11
sin el 40:  media 10.00   mediana 10

The mean moves 3.33 points and the median 1. A single value out of nine. That is why in uncleaned data the median is looked at first.

Step 4. Validate the input in one go

def check(frame: pd.DataFrame) -> list[str]:
    """Return everything wrong with this batch, instead of stopping at the first."""
    problems = []
    if not frame.grade.dropna().between(0, 100).all():
        problems.append("hay leyes fuera del rango 0 a 100")
    if frame.lot.duplicated().any():
        problems.append(f"identificadores repetidos: {frame.lot[frame.lot.duplicated()].tolist()}")
    unknown = set(frame.mineral.str.lower()) - {"oxido", "sulfuro", "mixto"}
    if unknown:
        problems.append(f"minerales no reconocidos: {sorted(unknown)}")
    return problems


for problem in check(lots):

Traps

Watch out

  • Statistics flags candidates, not culprits. The IQR criterion says what to look at; what to do with it is decided by whoever knows the process.
  • There are several quartile conventions. With the same nine numbers, the upper fence comes out 27 or 31. Say which one you used.
  • An extreme value moves the mean much more than the median. 3.33 points against 1, in the example. In dirty data, the median first.
  • A gap is not a zero, and treating it as zero lowers every mean without warning.
  • Before removing rows with missing values, compare that group with the rest. If it does not look alike, removing it changes whom the analysis describes.
  • Imputing shrinks variability. With many missing values it stops being harmless, and it is always declared.
  • Removing rows changes the denominator of every percentage you calculate afterwards.
  • duplicated() does not mark the first appearance. That is why the count is one less than people expect.
  • Normalise the text before counting categories, or oxido and OXIDO will be two things.
  • Never overwrite the original file. Cleaning produces a new file.
  • Anonymise before working, not before publishing. And remember that exact coordinates are someone's home.
Analogy

Metallurgical bridge

This whole module is the rejection criterion of an assay, which you already apply without calling it that.

When a laboratory analysis comes out wild, you neither delete it nor accept it: you investigate it. You look at whether the control standard was running fine that day, whether the sample was contaminated, whether the technician wrote percent where parts per thousand went. And only then you decide. That is exactly what the example of the 40% says.

A wild result in the seriesAn outlier candidate
Checking the control standard before rejectingInvestigating before deleting
A genuine high-grade veinThe extreme that has to be kept
Units confused on the sheetThe error that does get removed
Redoing the assayClipping to the value of the fence
An unlabelled sample that appears twiceThe duplicate that has to be investigated
The validity range of the methodInput validation

And there is a laboratory rule that is literally the module's: the original notebook is neither erased nor corrected over. The correction is noted next to it, with the date and who made it. That is "never overwrite the original file and write every decision with its count", in other words and a hundred years older.

Review

Review

With nine grades, numpy says the upper fence is at 27 and the calculation by hand at 31. Which is the correct one?

Both, because they are different conventions for calculating the quartiles and there are more than two. numpy interpolates and places Q1 at 7 and Q3 at 15; the method of taking the median of each half leaving out the central value gives 6 and 16, and since the IQR goes from 8 to 10, the fence moves from 27 to 31. With this data both flag only the 40, which is the usual thing, but a value of 29 would be an outlier with one and normal with the other. What cannot be done is to keep quiet about which one was used, because in the tight cases the verdict depends on that.

Why is it recommended to look at the median before the mean in uncleaned data?

Because a single extreme value moves it far less. In the module's example, removing the 40 from nine values changes the mean from 13.33 to 10.00, that is 3.33 points, while the median goes from 11 to 10, a single point. The mean shares the weight of every value equally, so a far-off value drags the result; the median only looks at who is in the centre. That property is what is called robustness, and that is why in the cleaning phase the median describes better what is there.

When can a row with missing data be removed and when not?

It can be when the data is missing at random, that is, when the probability of being missing does not depend on what the values would be, and besides they are few. The practical check is to compare the group that is missing the value with the rest: if they look alike, removing them biases nothing; if they do not, the absence carries information and removing them changes whom the analysis describes. When many are missing but at random, imputation is used, declaring with what and why, and knowing that imputing shrinks variability. And if a column is almost entirely missing, what gets removed is the column, not the rows.

Why should categories with no order not be encoded as 1, 2 and 3?

Because those numbers claim two things nobody has checked: that an order exists between the categories and that the distances between them are equal. Encoding oxide, sulphide and mixed as 1, 2 and 3 tells the model that mixed is triple oxide and that the difference between oxide and sulphide is the same as between sulphide and mixed. When there is no real order, one-hot is used, one column of ones and zeros per level minus one. Label encoding is only correct when the order genuinely exists, as in low, medium and high.

What is input validation and why is it done when reading the file?

It is checking that the data meets the rules it has to meet: plausible ranges, correct types, allowed values in the categories and uniqueness of the identifiers. It is done on reading because a problem detected there costs a minute, and the same problem detected when the result looks odd forces redoing the whole analysis to find out where it came from, if anyone notices at all. Written once as a function, it always runs, and it is best that it returns all the problems at once instead of stopping at the first, and that it says which specific record is wrong.
