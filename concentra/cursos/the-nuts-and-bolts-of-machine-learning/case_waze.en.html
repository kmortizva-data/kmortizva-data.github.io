Brief

The brief

Waze wants to know who is about to abandon the app so as to act beforehand. It is exactly the same brief as Course 4, and that repetition is what makes this project valuable.

Because Course 4 already built a model for this and left its numbers written down. So here something can be done that no stand-alone project can do: compare for real.

Question

The question

Does a tree model beat the logistic regression on this same problem?
Data

The data

waze_dataset.csv, 14,999 users. The 700 without a label are excluded just as in Course 4 and by the same check, so 14,299 remain, of whom 17.74% churn.

And the split is built so the comparison is exact, not approximate. The first cut is the same 25% with the same seed and the same stratification as Course 4:

PartUsersChurn
Training8,57917.74%
Validation2,14517.72%
Test3,57517.73%

Those 3,575 are literally the same users Course 4 set aside, and the 10,724 that project used whole for training are the ones shared out here between training and validation. Changing a single built variable would invalidate all of this, so exactly the same ones are built.

Control

The control, fixed before looking at anything

The Course 4 model, with its published figures: AUC 0.7368 and recall 0.0820 on those same 3,575 users.

And an expectation written before tuning anything, so it cannot be adjusted afterwards: the trees probably will not win. An untuned comparison already suggested it. This project tunes them seriously so the answer holds in both directions.

Cleaning

What had to be fixed

Nothing new relative to Course 4, and that is deliberate. The 700 missing labels, the division by zero of the kilometres per driving day and the built variables are identical.

What is new is the discipline. Course 4 split in two; here it is split in three and the test set takes part in no decision until the last step. In between, four families are compared and 88 knob combinations are tried, all against validation.

Exploration

What the exploration showed

Module 3 asked for grouping the users without labels, and the result is not the flattering one.

The best silhouette is 0.2588, with two groups, and no other number improves. Below 0.35 the groups touch each other: these users do not form separate families, they are a continuum.

The two groups that come out churn at 17.29% and at 19.05%, that is 1.75 points of difference. That is not an actionable segmentation.

It gets reported like that, and the variable goes into the model all the same, so that it is the model that says whether it was worth anything. Spoiler: it comes last of nine.

Model

The model, and why this model

Four families measured in validation, and a lone tree included on purpose to watch it lose:

ModelAUC in validation
Logistic regression0.7424
Boosting (xgboost)0.7273
Random forest0.7129
Lone tree0.5717

That 0.5717 of the lone tree is one step from tossing a coin, and that is why a forest cannot be judged by what a tree does. The two tree families get tuned with grid search and four-fold cross-validation:

ModelUntunedTunedWhat it gainedTime
Random forest0.71290.7405+0.027640 combinations, 61 s
Boosting (xgboost)0.72730.7401+0.012848 combinations, 13 s

Tuning the forest gave it 0.0276 of AUC, which is a big improvement for what is at stake here. And it stopped 0.0019 short of the logistic, which carries no knob to tune.

The champion was chosen in validation, before touching the test set, and it was the logistic.

No. The complicated model does not win, neither in validation nor in test.

And it is worth saying what the comparison does not prove: 0.7379 against 0.7348 is three thousandths over 3,575 users, and that does not survive a change of seed. What does survive is the qualitative conclusion, which is that the trees do not take off.

Result

The result, translated

Apart from the verdict, two things the model said on its own.

The activity days are more than half of the model, with 0.5163, more than the other eight variables together. And the module 3 segment comes last of nine, with 0.0026: the silhouette was already warning that there were no groups and the model confirms it from another place.

And the failure no model fixed: all four detect fewer than one in ten of those who leave. The best is this course's logistic with 0.0962 and the worst the tuned boosting with 0.0489.

Course 4 had already diagnosed that this was the threshold and not the model. Having three more models with recall stuck confirms it: it is not a limitation of the algorithm, it is where the cut is placed.

Decision

What gets decided with this

  1. Deploy the logistic regression, which wins and besides explains itself, can be defended in front of whoever it affects and gets fixed without retraining three hundred trees.
  2. Lower the threshold, which is the only thing that moves recall, accepting more false alarms. How far it gets lowered depends on what it costs to contact a user who was not leaving.
  3. Do not use the segmentation to target campaigns. The groups are not separated and barely differ in churn.
  4. Keep the forest as a diagnostic, not as a production model: its importance table is the one that says where to look.
Limits

Limitations

  • The differences are of thousandths. With 3,575 test users, 0.7379 against 0.7348 is not a difference that survives a change of seed.
  • The file does not say why anyone left. No model can predict well a cause that is not in the data, and there is the ceiling of this 0.74.
  • The Course 4 logistic was trained with 10,724 users and this one with 8,579, because here there is validation. Even with less data it came out slightly better, which confirms that the difference is within the noise.
  • The test set was looked at a single time, after choosing the champion. Everything else was decided against validation.
