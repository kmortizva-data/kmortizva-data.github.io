Brief

The brief

The New York City Taxi and Limousine Commission wants to know who leaves a generous tip, so the driver knows before accepting the ride.

And the ethical question goes before the model, not after. A system that tells the driver who is going to tip can end up deciding for whom the taxi stops. That is discrimination with the appearance of efficiency, and it has to be said even if the model works. Here it turns out it does not work either, but the order matters: the question was asked without yet knowing the result.

Question

The question

Can it be known, before the trip ends, whether the passenger is going to leave a generous tip?
Data

The data

C2_2017_Yellow_Taxi_Trip_Data.csv, 22,699 trips from 2017. The same ones as Course 2 and Course 4, with a completely different question.

Cleaning

What had to be fixed

Three things, and all three decided the project before training anything.

First, a leak checked and not assumed. total_amount includes the tip: verified by adding the parts and comparing with the total, it matches in 22,656 of 22,699 trips. That column contains the answer, so it is forbidden as a variable. The cost before the tip gets rebuilt by subtracting it, and that is indeed what the passenger sees on the screen when the prompt comes up.

Second, cash.

The meter does not record the cash tip. It is not that those people do not tip: it is that the device only writes down the card one.

Training with the whole file would teach the model "cash, therefore no tip", which is a property of the machine disguised as a finding, and would produce an excellent model for the wrong reason. The official exemplar does exactly that. Here 7,434 trips get set aside and it is said why, leaving 15,203 usable.

Third, what "generous" means, read from the distribution and not chosen.

Median card tip19.97%
Trips leaving exactly 20.00%4,363, 28.7%
Trips on one of the three buttons (20, 25, 30)43.1%

The distribution has three towers, and they are the three buttons the machine offers. Whoever presses the default button did not decide to be generous: they accepted. And the median falls right on that button, that is, half the passengers do not choose.

That is why the threshold is above 20%, and with it the generous ones are 23.11%.

Control

The control, fixed before looking at anything

With 23% positives, always answering "not generous" already gets 0.7688 of accuracy without doing absolutely anything. That is the bar everything else has to clear, and it is fixed before training.

And the expectation, written before tuning: tipping is a personal decision, and the columns of this file describe the trip. The likely thing is that it cannot be predicted, and if so the deliverable is saying it with evidence.

Model

The model, and why this model

Three-way split, 60/20/20 stratified: 9,121 for training, 3,041 for validation and 3,041 for test.

Four families in validation, and then grid search with four-fold cross-validation for the two tree ones: 24 combinations in 41 seconds for the forest and 36 in 11 for the boosting. The champion, chosen in validation, was the tuned boosting.

And the metric that decides is not accuracy. With 23% positives, accuracy rewards whoever flags nobody, so recall and AUC are looked at.

Comparison

The comparison

On the test set, looked at a single time, with the control inside:

The logistic and the forest flag absolutely nobody. They take exactly the same decisions as the lazy model, and that is why their accuracy coincides with its own down to the fourth decimal. The boosting detects 1.28% of the generous ones.

And here, for the first and only time in the course, the trees beat the logistic: 0.6073 and 0.6104 of AUC against 0.5395.

It does not mean what it seems. They rank the cases better, yes, but with the default threshold none flags anyone. It is winning a race they all lose.

A better AUC is not a usable model. The AUC measures whether you know how to rank; recall measures whether you are any use for deciding.
Result

The result, translated

Leaving a generous tip is a decision of the person, not of the trip. Neither the distance, nor the duration, nor the hour, nor what it cost predict who goes past the default button.

And that figure carries its own warning. A perfectly presentable split comes out, with the cost before the tip at the top with 0.3757. It is the importance split of a model with an AUC of 0.6073, that is, of a model that does not work.

Importance always adds up to 1 across the variables you give it, whether the model gets it right or not. Reading it without first checking whether the model works produces conclusions about nothing.

Decision

What gets decided with this

  1. Do not build this. The brief cannot be fulfilled with the data available, and saying so with evidence is the deliverable.
  2. If it were genuinely needed, what has to be obtained is information about the passenger, not more variables about the trip: tipping history, whether they are a regular customer, the app they booked through. None of that is in the file.
  3. Look at the default button, which is the real lever. 28.7% of passengers leave exactly what the machine proposes. **Moving that button moves more money than any model**, and it can be tested with an A/B experiment like those of Course 3.
  4. And do not deploy a system that tells the driver whom it pays to pick up, which was the risk declared at the start.
Limits

Limitations

  • One year, one city, and the file is a sample of the trips of 2017.
  • 7,434 trips are discarded for having no recorded tip. If whoever pays in cash behaves differently, this conclusion does not apply to them.
  • The 20% threshold is defensible but not the only one. With "going past 15%" the generous ones would be 77% and the problem would change shape.
  • The test set was looked at once, after choosing the champion in validation.
