Froth

Maps a scientific field by meaning, so a literature review stops being five cherry picked papers.

The problem
A literature review is meant to cover a field. In practice you read the twenty papers a keyword search happened to surface, and never find out what you missed.
How I solved it
Each paper becomes a vector by meaning, not by keyword (SPECTER). Density clustering finds the subtopics. Three views draw them: bubbles, a force directed network, and a reading guide.
The result
A field you can see the shape of: 44 named subtopics, the papers that bridge between them, and an honest count of what the map still leaves unconnected.

Measured

44 subtopics from one reading of the whole corpus

Every paper placed by meaning, the subtopics named, and the papers that bridge two of them marked. What the map leaves unconnected is counted on screen instead of hidden: at similarity 0.75 it is 42% of the drawn papers, and that number is part of the product.

Starting a literature review means reading the five papers someone recommended and hoping they were the right five. Froth reads the whole field first and shows you its shape: which subtopics exist, which papers sit between two of them, and where the corpus is thin.

Every threshold inside is recalculated for each corpus rather than shipped as a default, so the same tool behaves the same on a field it has never seen.
