bg-remover

Cuts the background out of a photo on your own machine, and lets you repair the edges the model got wrong.

The problem
Cutting a background out usually means uploading your photo to someone else's server, and then accepting whatever edge the model decided on.
How I solved it
A segmentation model returns only the alpha mask, never a finished cutout. The browser holds the original pixels, the model's mask and a working copy as three separate layers.
The result
A cutout that never leaves your machine, and two brushes that actually fix the edges the model got wrong.

Measured

the first cut takes 13 to 15 seconds, every one after that takes 2

DirectML compiles its GPU shaders on the first inference. Nothing is broken, but a user cannot tell a fifteen second wait from a hang, so the overlay counts the seconds out loud instead of spinning silently.

The design decision worth explaining is why the server sends back a greyscale mask instead of a finished cutout.

If it sent a finished RGBA image, the colour under the transparent pixels would be gone. Many libraries write black there. A Restore brush would then have nothing real to bring back, only black. So the browser keeps the uploaded pixels untouched, keeps the model's mask untouched as the ground truth, and paints only on a third mutable copy.

That is what makes Restore behave correctly. It does not force a pixel to full opacity, it copies back whatever the model originally predicted. A wisp of hair the model judged 60% transparent returns to 60%, instead of turning into a hard opaque blob. That single detail is the difference between a brush that repairs an edge and one that ruins it.

It runs on DirectML rather than CUDA, because getting CUDA working for this GPU generation on Windows cost more than it was worth, and it logs which execution provider actually ended up active rather than assuming the GPU won.
