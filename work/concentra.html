All work

4 courses, 21 lessons. Course 5 in progress.

Concentra

Turns the public syllabus of a course into a study sheet you can actually consult later.

Feed
The publicly visible syllabus of a Coursera course. One request, cached, nothing behind the login.
Separation
The module map is generated from the syllabus, never typed by hand. Around it I write the glossary, the recipe, the pitfalls and the review questions, with analogies from metallurgy.
Concentrate
One self contained HTML sheet per module, offline, dark and light, and it prints to PDF cleanly.

Assay

23 rounds of correction for the first course alone

Course 3 took 23 passes before it was right. The procedure that came out of that is written down in MANUAL.md, and three automated checks now have to pass before anything is committed. Skipping it means doing the work three times.

I pay for the courses, watch them, and forget them. There was nowhere to look things up afterwards in a condensed form, so I built one.

The metaphor is deliberate and it matches Froth: the course is the feed, the cheatsheet is the concentrate.

The rule I care about most here is where the boundary sits. Only the public course description page is ever requested, once per course. Nothing behind the login is downloaded, no video is touched, and the cheatsheets are notes rewritten from scratch rather than a copy of the course. The cached page is Coursera's markup, so it stays out of the repository.

The site itself has no dependencies at all, just the Python standard library, which means it still builds on a machine where installing anything is slow.
